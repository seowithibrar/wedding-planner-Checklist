## Wedding Planning Priorities 6 Months Out: What to Book First for a Wedding

Anyone asking what to book first for a wedding on a short timeline needs the same answer: the items that cannot move and have the fewest alternatives. These are your wedding planning priorities 6 months out, in the order your 6-month wedding planning checklist should follow.

- **Budget and guest count.** Every other choice depends on them. Agree on a total, who contributes, and a rough headcount in the first few days.
- **Venue.** Capacity, date and catering rules narrow everything else. Book the venue first, because a venue without your date makes the rest pointless.
- **Photographer.** Strong photographers book popular dates early. Ask if they are free on your date and whether they will shoot the wedding personally.
- **Caterer, or confirmation of venue catering.** If the venue includes catering, confirm the package. If not, book a caterer next.
- **Officiant or registrar, and the legal paperwork.** Check what your local rules require and how long a license stays valid.
- **Attire.** Pick something available now, not something that has to be made.
- **Music, florals, hair and makeup.** These come after the first six and still have good availability at six months.

## Wedding Planning Checklist 24 Weeks: Month by Month

This section is the heart of the 6-month wedding planning checklist. It is a wedding planning month by month 6 months plan, written as wedding planning tasks by month. Each block of the 6-month wedding planning checklist has a short goal and a list you can tick off. The dates in the example are for a wedding on Saturday, 3 April 2027, so day one is 3 October 2026. Swap in your own date and the pattern holds.

### Wedding Planning Checklist Month 1: Lock the Backbone (6 to 5 Months Out)

Goal: Know your budget and guest count, and book the venue and photographer. The first two weeks matter more than anything after them.

- ☐ Set your total budget and agree who is contributing and how much
- ☐ Settle on a rough guest count, split into a must-invite list and a maybe list
- ☐ List two or three possible dates, including Fridays and Sundays
- ☐ Contact venues and book one within the first two weeks
- ☐ Book your photographer and confirm they are free on your date
- ☐ Decide if you will hire a planner or a day-of coordinator
- ☐ Create a shared email address for quotes, contracts and receipts
- ☐ Open a simple tracker with columns for task, owner, deadline, cost and status
- ☐ Start a [wedding planning dashboard](/tools/wedding-planning-dashboard) so every deadline lives in one place

Tip: If your first-choice venue is booked, ask which nearby dates are free. A Friday evening or Sunday afternoon can open up a venue that is full on Saturdays, and it is often cheaper.

### Wedding Planning Checklist Month 2: Core Vendors and Attire (5 to 4 Months Out)

Goal: Book the vendors who shape the day and choose what you will wear.

- ☐ Book your caterer, or confirm what the venue's catering package includes
- ☐ Hire a DJ or band and check their availability for your date
- ☐ Book a florist, or choose a decor plan that fits the venue
- ☐ Choose attire that can be bought now: ready-to-wear, sample or rental
- ☐ Book alterations at the same time as the purchase
- ☐ Reserve hotel room blocks for out-of-town guests
- ☐ Build a simple wedding website with date, location, travel and stay details
- ☐ Open a gift registry if you want one
- ☐ Add every deposit and balance due date to your tracker

### Wedding Planning Checklist Month 3: Guests and Style (4 to 3 Months Out)

Goal: Get the guest layer moving and finish the second round of vendors.

- ☐ Finalize the guest list and collect addresses
- ☐ Order or design invitations and send them around 10 to 12 weeks out
- ☐ Ask your wedding party and tell them what you need from them
- ☐ Buy wedding rings, since engraving or sizing can take weeks
- ☐ Book hair and makeup, and schedule a trial
- ☐ Book transportation if the venue is remote or parking is limited
- ☐ Book the rehearsal dinner venue
- ☐ Order bridal party attire in styles that are in stock
- ☐ Confirm every booked vendor has a signed contract and a paid deposit

### Wedding Planning Checklist Month 4: Details and Logistics (3 to 2 Months Out)

Goal: Move from big bookings to the details guests will notice.

- ☐ Track RSVPs as they arrive, including meal and dietary choices
- ☐ Attend your menu tasting and confirm selections with the caterer
- ☐ Apply for your marriage license, checking how long it stays valid
- ☐ Order the cake or desserts and ask about cutting fees
- ☐ Plan the ceremony with your officiant: readings, vows and music
- ☐ Reserve rentals such as chairs, linens and lighting
- ☐ Book honeymoon travel and check passport validity
- ☐ Start a seating chart draft using your guest list

### Wedding Planning Checklist Month 5: Confirm Everything (2 to 1 Month Out)

Goal: Check every detail and make sure each vendor has the same information.

- ☐ Attend dress or suit fittings and bring the shoes and accessories
- ☐ Draft the wedding day timeline and send it to vendors for feedback
- ☐ Call each vendor to confirm date, arrival time, venue address and contact
- ☐ Brief your wedding party in writing on where to be and when
- ☐ Prepare gratuity envelopes and a payment schedule
- ☐ Write personal vows if you are using them
- ☐ Pack a bridal emergency kit using our [bridal emergency kit checklist](/checklists/bridal-emergency-kit-checklist)
- ☐ Finalize the playlist and any must-play or do-not-play songs

### Wedding Planning Checklist Month 6: Execute the Plan (Final 4 Weeks)

Goal: Stop making new decisions. Confirm, hand off and enjoy the final weeks.

- ☐ Give the caterer your final headcount, meal counts and allergy notes
- ☐ Finalize the seating chart and print place cards
- ☐ Make the final payments and prepare tips
- ☐ Hand the finished timeline to your coordinator or point person
- ☐ Pack for the honeymoon
- ☐ Deliver decor, signs and personal items to the venue
- ☐ Attend the rehearsal and the rehearsal dinner
- ☐ Rest, eat well and let your plan do the work

That completes the monthly tasks in your 6-month wedding planning checklist. After the wedding, send thank-you notes, return rentals and leave vendor reviews while details are still fresh.

## Wedding Planning Checklist with Budget: How to Handle Money in 6 Months

Adding a budget column to your 6-month wedding planning checklist keeps costs from creeping up while you are moving fast. On a short timeline you have less room to shop around, so the budget needs to be set before the first booking.

- **Set the total first.** Decide the number you're comfortable spending, plus what each contributor is giving, before you pay any deposit.
- **Divide by category.** Split the total across venue and catering, photography, attire, music, florals, stationery, transport and a buffer.
- **Log every deposit.** Record the amount, date, balance and due date the moment you pay.
- **Keep a buffer.** Rush fees, alterations and extra headcount are common on a short timeline.
- **Review weekly.** Compare the tracker with your category budget once a week.

A wedding of 50 guests opens more venues, including restaurants, galleries, private dining rooms and small estates. With 100 guests, the venue and caterer become the central decisions. Book the venue in week 1 or 2, request catering quotes in the same week, and plan on rentals for tables, chairs and linens. ### 6 Month Wedding Timeline for Destination Wedding

A 6 month wedding timeline for destination wedding plans needs travel dates first. Choose the place, confirm legal requirements for marrying there, and book the venue and a local planner in the first two weeks. ### 6 Month Wedding Timeline for Courthouse Wedding

A 6 month wedding timeline for courthouse wedding plans starts with the legal side: confirm appointment rules, documents, witnesses and fees with your local office. For a 6 month wedding timeline for backyard wedding plans, you take on what a venue usually provides. ## Realistic 6 Month Wedding Timeline: What to Skip and What to Simplify

A realistic 6 month wedding timeline means giving up some things, and every good 6-month wedding planning checklist reflects that. This is a short list of reasonable trade-offs that save time without hurting the day.

- Skip formal save-the-dates. Text your closest guests and put the date on your wedding website.
- Choose ready-to-wear or sample attire. Custom pieces often need months of production plus alterations.
- Shorten the comparison phase. Get two or three quotes per vendor, then book.
- Pick simpler stationery. Digital invitations are faster, and plain printed ones avoid long production times.
- Use a DJ if a live band is booked. DJs often have more open dates at short notice.
- Be flexible on the day of the week. A Friday or Sunday wedding widens your options.

## Common Mistakes on a 6-Month Wedding Planning Checklist

These are the slips that most often derail a 6-month wedding planning checklist.

- Shopping for the venue for weeks. Visit a few and decide. The longer you wait, the fewer dates remain.
- Ordering attire that must be made. Check delivery dates in writing before you pay.
- Forgetting the guest list until late. Without a count, you can't finalize catering or seating. The [guest list manager](/tools/guest-list-manager) keeps it organized.
- Handling everything alone. Assign tasks to your partner, parents and wedding party.
- Skipping contracts. Every booking needs a signed agreement and a paid deposit.
- Ignoring the paperwork. Check marriage license rules early, since waiting periods and validity windows vary.

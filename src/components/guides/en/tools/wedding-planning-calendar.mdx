## What Is a Wedding Planning Calendar?

A wedding planning calendar is the place where every date-bound part of your wedding lives: venue tours, cake tastings, dress fittings, payment deadlines, RSVP cut-offs and the final week of appointments. A checklist tells you **what** needs to happen. A calendar tells you **when**, and it shows you where two commitments are about to collide.

This free calendar has three views that work together:

- **Month view** for appointments and the big goals you want to reach that month.
- **Week view** for weekly priorities and a short task list for each day.
- **Day view** for busy days, split into morning, afternoon and evening blocks.

Everything you enter is saved automatically in your browser, so you can close the page and pick up where you left off on the same device.

## How to Use the Wedding Planning Calendar

### Step 1: Add every fixed appointment first

Start in the month view. Add each appointment you have already booked: venue walkthroughs, vendor calls, tastings, fittings, hair and makeup trials and the rehearsal. Give each one a time, a category such as venue, catering or attire, and a short note such as "bring swatches" or "pay second deposit".

Fixed appointments are the skeleton of your plan. Once they are on the calendar, you can see which weeks are already full and which ones still have room for flexible tasks.

### Step 2: Set one to three milestone goals per month

Below the month grid, add the milestones that month must deliver. Good monthly goals are outcomes, not activities:

- "Venue contract signed" rather than "look at venues".
- "Save-the-dates mailed" rather than "work on stationery".
- "Final guest count sent to caterer" rather than "chase RSVPs".

Tick a goal off when it is done. If a goal keeps sliding to the next month, that is a signal to simplify it, delegate it or book help.

### Step 3: Plan the coming week every weekend

Switch to the week view once a week, ideally on the same evening each time. Add up to three priorities for the week, then give each day one or two small tasks. Keep weekday tasks short, around 20 to 30 minutes, so planning fits around work. Save bigger jobs such as DIY projects or venue visits for the weekend.

The progress ring shows how much of the week is complete. If you regularly finish less than half, you are planning too much per week, not working too slowly.

### Step 4: Use the day view for heavy days

Some days carry several appointments: a tasting in the morning, a florist meeting after lunch and a call with the officiant in the evening. Open the day view and place tasks in the morning, afternoon or evening block. This shows you whether the day is realistic and whether you have left travel time between stops.

### Step 5: Print and review

Use the print button to keep a paper copy of the month on the fridge or in your wedding binder. Review the calendar with your partner for ten minutes each week. A short, regular review prevents the classic surprise of a deposit due tomorrow.

## What to Put on Your Wedding Calendar

Use this table as a starting list of dated items. Most couples are surprised by how many there are.

| Category | Dates worth adding |
| --- | --- |
| Venue | Tours, contract signing, deposit and balance due dates, final walkthrough |
| Vendors | Discovery calls, contract deadlines, payment schedule, final confirmation calls |
| Catering | Tasting, menu decision deadline, final headcount due date |
| Attire | Shopping appointments, each fitting, final pick-up |
| Beauty | Hair and makeup trial, skin and nail appointments |
| Stationery | Save-the-date mailing, invitation mailing, RSVP deadline |
| Legal | Marriage licence appointment and its validity window |
| Guests | Hotel block release date, welcome event, rehearsal dinner |
| Final week | Vendor hand-off, rehearsal, item drop-off at venue, wedding day |

Payment deadlines deserve special attention. Add each one as its own entry with the amount in the notes, so a balance due never hides inside a vendor's contract.

## How Far Ahead Should You Plan Each View?

| View | How far ahead | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Month | The whole engagement | Booked appointments and one to three outcomes per month |
| Week | The next one to two weeks | Three priorities and small daily tasks |
| Day | Only for busy days | Time-blocked tasks for morning, afternoon and evening |

Detailed daily planning months in advance rarely survives contact with real vendor replies. Plan in broad strokes far ahead and in detail close up.

## Sample Calendar Rhythm for a 12-Month Engagement

- **Months 12 to 10:** venue tours, photographer and planner calls, budget meeting with family. Goal: venue and date locked.
- **Months 9 to 7:** caterer and band or DJ meetings, attire shopping, engagement photos. Goal: core vendors booked.
- **Months 6 to 4:** save-the-dates, florist and cake tastings, first fitting. Goal: invitations designed.
- **Months 3 to 2:** invitations mailed, menu finalised, hair and makeup trial, marriage licence. Goal: RSVPs collected.
- **Final month:** final fittings, seating plan, vendor confirmations, rehearsal. Goal: everything confirmed in writing.

## Tips for Keeping Your Wedding Calendar Useful

- **Book planning time like an appointment.** A recurring weekly slot protects planning from being pushed aside by everything else.
- **Add buffers.** Leave a gap of at least a day between a vendor deadline and the date you actually need the item.
- **Group errands by area.** Put appointments in the same neighbourhood on the same day to save travel.
- **Protect rest days.** Leave at least one weekend day a month free of wedding tasks. Burnout slows planning more than any vendor.
- **Write the next action in the notes.** "Email florist the final colour palette" is easier to act on than "florist".

## Common Calendar Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Only adding appointments.** Deadlines matter just as much. Add RSVP dates, payment dates and the date your final headcount is due.
2. **Planning every day in detail.** Over-detailed plans break quickly. Plan the week, not the month, at task level.
3. **Forgetting the marriage licence window.** Many licences are valid only for a set number of days. Put both the appointment and the expiry date on the calendar.
4. **Ignoring travel time.** Back-to-back appointments across town lead to rushed decisions.
5. **Keeping it to yourself.** Share a printed copy with your partner, planner or the family member helping you, so everyone works from the same dates.

## Why Booking Order Matters

The best vendors in your area can only work one wedding per day. If you book in the wrong order, you may lose your favourite photographer while you are still choosing flowers. Booking in the right order also protects your budget, because each early booking tells you how much is left for the rest.

## Vendor Booking Order and Timing

| Order | Vendor | When to book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Venue | 12 to 18 months before |
| 2 | Planner or coordinator | 12 to 15 months before |
| 3 | Photographer and videographer | 10 to 14 months before |
| 4 | Caterer (if not provided by venue) | 9 to 12 months before |
| 5 | Band or DJ | 9 to 12 months before |
| 6 | Officiant | 9 to 12 months before |
| 7 | Florist | 6 to 9 months before |
| 8 | Hair and makeup | 6 to 9 months before |
| 9 | Cake or dessert baker | 4 to 6 months before |
| 10 | Stationery | 4 to 6 months before |
| 11 | Rentals and lighting | 4 to 6 months before |
| 12 | Transport | 3 to 4 months before |

On a short timeline, book the first six in the same week. Our [3-month wedding planning checklist](/checklists/3-month-wedding-planning-checklist) shows how.

## Before You Contact Any Vendor

- Know your date, or two or three possible dates.
- Know your guest count and venue.
- Set a budget for each category using the [Wedding Budget Calculator](/tools/budget-calculator).
- Collect a few inspiration images to share.
- Prepare a short list of must-have requirements for each vendor.

## Questions to Ask Every Vendor

1. Are you available on our date?
2. What is included in your packages, and what costs extra?
3. Who exactly will work at our wedding? Will it be you or a member of your team?
4. How long have you worked weddings like ours, at venues like ours?
5. Can we see full examples of past weddings, not only highlights?
6. What are your deposit, payment and cancellation terms?
7. Do you charge for travel, overtime, setup or removal?
8. Do you need a meal, a break or a place to rest?
9. Do you have insurance, and can you provide proof if the venue requires it?
10. What happens if you are ill or cannot attend on the day?

## Contract Checklist

Before you sign, make sure each contract states:

- [ ] Your names, the wedding date, venue address and start and end times
- [ ] A detailed list of services, items and quantities
- [ ] The total price, including taxes and service charges
- [ ] The deposit amount and whether it is refundable
- [ ] The payment schedule with due dates
- [ ] Overtime rates and how overtime is approved
- [ ] Cancellation and rescheduling terms for both sides
- [ ] What happens if the vendor cannot attend
- [ ] Meal, break, parking and power requirements
- [ ] Delivery, setup and removal times
- [ ] Insurance and liability details
- [ ] Usage rights for photos and videos, where relevant
- [ ] The name of the person who will be your main contact

Ask for any verbal promises to be added in writing before you sign.

## Red Flags When Booking Vendors

- No written contract, or a contract with blank sections.
- Pressure to pay the full amount immediately.
- Only payment in cash with no receipt.
- No reviews, or reviews mentioning no-shows or late delivery.
- Unwillingness to show complete examples of past work.
- Vague answers about who will work on the day.

## Final Month Vendor Confirmations

- Confirm arrival times, setup windows and contact numbers.
- Send the final day-of timeline and venue floor plan.
- Confirm final guest numbers with the caterer and venue.
- Share the shot list with the photographer and the song list with the DJ.
- Confirm final payments and tips.
- Give your coordinator a contact sheet for every vendor.

## After the Wedding

- Pay any remaining balances promptly.
- Return rented items on time.
- Leave reviews for vendors who did a great job.
- Check the timeline for photo and video delivery.

## How to Compare Vendor Quotes

Quotes rarely use the same format, so compare them on the same points:

| Point to compare | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Hours of service | Six hours and eight hours are very different prices |
| Staff on the day | One photographer or two, one chef or a full team |
| Items included | Delivery, setup, removal, equipment and rentals |
| Extra fees | Travel, overtime, late-night and holiday surcharges |
| Taxes and service charges | Can add 20 percent or more to the headline price |
| Payment terms | Deposit size, refundability and balance due dates |
| Reviews and references | Recent couples at similar venues |

Use a spreadsheet or the [Wedding Budget Calculator](/tools/budget-calculator) to see the full price of each option side by side.

## How to Negotiate Politely

- Ask what can be included or removed, rather than asking for a discount.
- Ask whether off-peak dates, weekdays or shorter hours change the price.
- Bundle services with one vendor, such as photo and video, where it makes sense.
- Be honest about your budget early, so vendors can suggest suitable packages.
- Get every agreed change in writing before paying the deposit.

## What to Send Vendors After Booking

- A copy of the signed contract and receipt for the deposit.
- The venue address, access details and parking instructions.
- Your planner or coordinator's name and phone number.
- Any inspiration images or song lists they asked for.
- A reminder of when you will send the final timeline and headcount.

## Common Vendor Booking Mistakes

1. **Booking before the budget is set.** Early overspending reduces options later.
2. **Comparing quotes that are not like for like.** Check hours, items and staff.
3. **Not reading the cancellation terms.** Know what happens if plans change.
4. **Forgetting vendor meals and overtime.** Both add to the final bill.
5. **No single contact list.** Your coordinator needs every number on the day.

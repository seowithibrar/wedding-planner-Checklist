## How to Use This 12-Month Wedding Planning Checklist

A 12-month engagement is long enough to compare options and short enough that you cannot leave the big bookings for later. The key is front-loading: the decisions that limit everything else, such as budget, guest count, date and venue, belong in the first quarter. After that, the plan moves from booking to designing and then to confirming.

Work through the checklist month by month. Tick tasks off in the [Wedding Planning Dashboard](/tools/wedding-planning-dashboard), and add every appointment and payment date to the [Wedding Planning Calendar](/tools/wedding-planning-calendar) as soon as it is booked.

## The 12-Month Plan at a Glance

| Months before | Focus | Main outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 | Foundations | Budget, guest estimate and wedding style agreed |
| 11 to 10 | Big bookings | Venue, date, photographer and planner booked |
| 9 to 8 | Core vendors | Caterer, music, officiant and attire shopping |
| 7 to 6 | Guests and design | Save-the-dates, website, florist and room blocks |
| 5 to 4 | Details | Cake, stationery, rentals, rings and transport |
| 3 | Invitations | Invitations mailed, trials booked, menu tasting |
| 2 | Confirmation | Marriage licence, RSVPs, seating plan, fittings |
| 1 | Final details | Final payments, timeline, vendor confirmations, rehearsal |

## Month 12: Lay the Foundations

- Talk about the kind of wedding you both want: size, formality, season and location.
- Agree on a total budget and who is contributing. Enter it into the [Budget Calculator](/tools/budget-calculator) to see a category split.
- Draft a rough guest list to estimate your guest count. Our [guest list organizer checklist](/checklists/wedding-guest-list-organizer) shows how to tier it.
- Choose two or three possible dates or a preferred season.
- Decide whether you will hire a full planner, a day-of coordinator or plan yourselves.
- Start a shared folder or binder for quotes, contracts and inspiration.

## Months 11 to 10: Book the Venue and the Vendors Who Sell Out First

- Shortlist and tour venues, ideally at the same time of day as your ceremony. Use our guide on [how to choose the perfect wedding venue](/blog/how-to-choose-wedding-venue) for questions to ask.
- Sign the venue contract and pay the deposit.
- Book your photographer and, if wanted, videographer.
- Book a planner or coordinator if you are using one.
- Consider an engagement shoot with your photographer.
- Buy wedding insurance if it suits your plans, especially for outdoor or destination weddings.

## Months 9 to 8: Core Vendors and Attire

- Book the caterer if the venue does not provide one, and the bar service if needed.
- Book a band or DJ, and ceremony musicians.
- Confirm your officiant and discuss the ceremony style.
- Choose your wedding party and tell them what you will ask of them.
- Start shopping for the wedding dress or outfit. Allow time for ordering and alterations.
- Book hair and makeup artists for the date, even if trials come later.

## Months 7 to 6: Guests, Website and Design

- Finalise the guest list and collect addresses.
- Build a wedding website with travel and accommodation information.
- Send save-the-dates.
- Reserve hotel room blocks for out-of-town guests.
- Book the florist and agree on a colour palette. A [wedding moodboard](/blog/moodboard-layout) helps vendors understand your style.
- Shop for the wedding party's outfits and partner's attire.
- Start a gift registry if you want one.

## Months 5 to 4: Details and Rentals

- Order the wedding cake or dessert table after a tasting.
- Order invitations and other stationery: menus, place cards and signage.
- Book rentals such as chairs, linens, lighting or a tent.
- Arrange transport for the couple, wedding party and, if needed, guests.
- Buy wedding rings and allow time for sizing or engraving.
- Plan the rehearsal dinner and any welcome event.
- Book your honeymoon if you are taking one soon after the wedding.

## Month 3: Invitations and Trials

- Mail formal invitations 8 to 10 weeks before the wedding.
- Schedule hair and makeup trials and your nail appointments.
- Hold the menu tasting and choose the final menu.
- Write the ceremony outline with your officiant.
- Buy accessories: shoes, veil, jewellery and undergarments for fittings.
- Plan favours, welcome bags and gifts for the wedding party and parents.

## Month 2: Licence, RSVPs and Seating

- Check the marriage licence rules where you will marry and book an appointment within the valid window.
- Track RSVPs and follow up with guests who have not replied after the deadline.
- Begin the seating chart once most replies are in.
- Attend dress or suit fittings.
- Write your vows if you are writing your own.
- Share a draft day-of timeline with key vendors.

## Month 1: Confirm Everything

- Give the final headcount to the caterer and venue by the contract deadline.
- Finalise the seating chart, escort cards and place cards.
- Confirm arrival times, contacts and final details with every vendor in writing.
- Make final payments and prepare cash envelopes for tips.
- Build the detailed [wedding day-of timeline](/checklists/wedding-day-of-timeline-checklist).
- Pack your [bridal emergency kit](/checklists/bridal-emergency-kit-checklist).
- Hold the rehearsal and hand off items to your coordinator or a trusted friend.

## Typical Payment Schedule Over 12 Months

Most vendors ask for a deposit to hold your date and the balance close to the wedding. Plan cash flow so large payments do not land in the same month:

| When | What is usually paid |
| --- | --- |
| Months 11 to 10 | Venue and photographer deposits |
| Months 9 to 6 | Caterer, music, florist and attire deposits |
| Months 5 to 3 | Cake, rentals, stationery, rings |
| Final 4 weeks | Venue and caterer balances, final vendor balances and tips |

## Common Mistakes on a 12-Month Timeline

1. **Waiting too long to book the venue.** Everything else depends on the date. Aim to sign within the first two to three months.
2. **Booking vendors before the budget is set.** Without category limits, early bookings can take money meant for later.
3. **Ordering the dress late.** Made-to-order gowns plus alterations can take six months or more.
4. **Forgetting the marriage licence window.** Licences are often valid for a limited period, so timing matters.
5. **Leaving the seating chart to the last week.** Start it as soon as most RSVPs are in.

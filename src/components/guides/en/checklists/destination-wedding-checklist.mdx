## Planning a Wedding Away From Home

A destination wedding turns your celebration into a shared trip. It can be more relaxed and memorable than a wedding at home, but it adds three challenges: legal paperwork in another country, guest travel, and managing vendors you may never meet before the week of the wedding. This checklist addresses each one.

## Destination Wedding Timeline

| When | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 18 to 14 months | Choose the destination, budget and guest list size |
| 14 to 12 months | Book the venue or resort and local planner; check legal requirements |
| 12 to 9 months | Save-the-dates, wedding website, room blocks |
| 9 to 6 months | Site visit, key vendors, attire, travel bookings |
| 6 to 3 months | Invitations, welcome events, excursions, legal documents |
| 3 to 1 months | Final numbers, transfers, welcome bags, packing |
| Wedding week | Arrive early, meet vendors, rehearse, enjoy |

## Choosing the Destination

- Consider travel time and cost for most of your guests.
- Check the weather and the hurricane or monsoon season for your date.
- Look at visa and passport requirements for your guests.
- Compare the cost of accommodation and food at different price levels.
- Think about accessibility for older guests or those with mobility needs.
- Check whether you can legally marry there and how long you must be in the country beforehand.

## Legal Requirements

Legal rules vary widely. Check with the destination's civil registry or consulate and your local registry office:

- Required documents, such as passports, birth certificates and proof of single status.
- Whether documents need translation, certification or an apostille.
- Residency requirements before the ceremony.
- Blood tests or medical certificates, where required.
- Whether you need witnesses and how many.
- How to register the marriage in your home country afterwards.

Many couples choose a short legal ceremony at home before travelling, so the destination ceremony can be symbolic and free of paperwork.

## Booking the Venue and Local Team

- Choose an all-inclusive resort for simpler planning, or a private villa or venue for more flexibility.
- Ask about exclusivity: will other weddings happen on the same day?
- Book a local planner or confirm the resort's coordinator.
- Ask for the vendor list and whether outside vendors are allowed.
- Confirm the rain plan and any seasonal weather risks.
- Read the contract for cancellation, rescheduling and force majeure terms.

## Guest Travel and Accommodation

- Negotiate a room block or group rate and note the booking deadline.
- Share flight options, airport details and transfer information on your wedding website.
- Remind guests to check passport validity, as many countries require several months remaining.
- Recommend travel insurance.
- Arrange airport transfers or shuttles.
- Provide a schedule of the weekend: welcome drinks, ceremony, reception and farewell brunch.

## The Wedding Website Is Essential

Your website becomes the travel guide for your guests. Include:

- Dates, venue address and the full weekend schedule.
- Accommodation options with booking links and deadlines.
- Travel information: airports, transfers, visas and currency.
- Dress code for each event, with weather notes.
- Local tips: things to do, safety and health advice.
- RSVP deadline and how to contact you.

## Vendor Planning From a Distance

- Schedule video calls with key vendors.
- Ask for references from couples who married at the same venue.
- Get every detail in writing, including timings, menus and decor.
- Consider bringing your own photographer if you have a favourite, but check permits and travel costs.
- Plan a site visit six to nine months before to meet vendors and taste the menu, if possible.

## Packing and Logistics

- Carry the wedding dress or outfit in hand luggage, never in checked bags.
- Pack rings, legal documents and vows in your carry-on.
- Check rules for bringing decor, favours or alcohol into the country.
- Bring adaptors, chargers and a printed copy of all contracts and contacts.
- Pack an emergency kit. Our [bridal emergency kit checklist](/checklists/bridal-emergency-kit-checklist) lists everything.

## Welcome Bags and Guest Experience

- Include a printed weekend schedule, local map and emergency contacts.
- Add practical items: water, sunscreen, snacks, insect repellent or a fan.
- Plan one group activity, such as a welcome dinner or excursion.
- Arrange a farewell brunch so guests can say goodbye.

## Budget Considerations

| Cost area | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Venue and catering | Often packaged at resorts, priced per guest |
| Local planner | Fee or percentage; often worth it for distance planning |
| Legal and documents | Translations, certifications and fees |
| Your travel | Flights, accommodation for several nights, site visit |
| Guest hosting | Welcome events, transfers, welcome bags |
| Photographer travel | Flights and accommodation if bringing your own |

Track these items in the [Wedding Budget Calculator](/tools/budget-calculator) so travel costs do not surprise you.

## Guest Communication Timeline

| When | What to send |
| --- | --- |
| 9 to 12 months before | Save-the-date with destination, dates and website link |
| 6 to 8 months before | Room block details and booking deadline |
| 3 to 4 months before | Formal invitation and RSVP |
| 6 to 8 weeks before | Weekend schedule, transfer details, dress codes |
| 1 to 2 weeks before | Final reminders, weather forecast, emergency contacts |

Keep messages short and link to your wedding website for full details.

## Hosting Etiquette for Destination Weddings

- Invite every guest to the welcome event and farewell brunch if possible.
- Give guests free time; do not plan every hour of the trip.
- Thank guests for travelling with a small gift or welcome bag.
- Be understanding when guests decline because of cost or time.

## Arrival Week Checklist

- Meet your local planner and walk through the venue together.
- Confirm final numbers, menus and timings in person.
- Check the weather forecast and confirm the rain plan.
- Collect any legal documents you need from local offices.
- Greet guests as they arrive and share a printed weekend schedule.

## Common Destination Wedding Mistakes

1. **Sending save-the-dates too late.** Guests need time to save and request leave.
2. **Ignoring the weather season.** Storm and rain seasons can affect your date.
3. **Leaving legal paperwork to the end.** Document processing can take months.
4. **Not checking for other weddings.** Ask for exclusivity or timing details.
5. **Packing the dress in checked luggage.** Always carry it on.

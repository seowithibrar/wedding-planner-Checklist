## Why a Day-Of Timeline Matters

On the wedding day, dozens of people need to be in the right place at the right moment: hair and makeup artists, photographers, the caterer, the DJ, the officiant, the wedding party and family. A clear, shared timeline keeps everyone in step and frees you from answering questions all day.

## How to Build Your Timeline

1. **Start with the fixed times.** Ceremony start, venue access, dinner service and the venue's end time.
2. **Work backwards for the morning.** Count hair and makeup time, dressing, travel and photos.
3. **Work forwards for the evening.** Plan the reception order, using the [wedding reception planning checklist](/checklists/wedding-reception-planning-checklist).
4. **Add buffers.** Leave 10 to 15 minutes between key moments.
5. **Add names.** Write who is responsible for each moment.
6. **Share and confirm.** Send it to every vendor and ask for corrections.

## Morning Checklist

- Eat a proper breakfast and drink water.
- Keep the getting-ready space tidy for photos.
- Lay out details for the photographer: rings, invitation, shoes, jewellery and perfume.
- Give the emergency kit to a trusted person. Use our [bridal emergency kit checklist](/checklists/bridal-emergency-kit-checklist).
- Confirm transport pick-up times.
- Hand rings and the marriage licence to the right people.

## Ceremony Checklist

- Ushers and seating plan for reserved rows.
- Ceremony music cues for the processional, signing and recessional.
- Microphone for the officiant and readers.
- Rings with the best man or ring bearer.
- Marriage licence, pen and witnesses.
- Someone to cue the wedding party to walk.

## Photo Plan

- Give the photographer a shot list with names of family groups.
- Assign a family member who knows everyone to gather people for photos.
- Keep family photo groups short, starting with the largest groups.
- Plan couple portraits at the best light, often just before sunset.

For pose ideas, read our [wedding photography poses for beginners](/blog/wedding-photography-poses-for-beginners).

## Who Gets the Timeline?

| Person | What they need most |
| --- | --- |
| Coordinator | The full timeline and every vendor's contact |
| Venue manager | Access, setup, dinner and end times |
| Photographer and videographer | Locations, key moments and photo groups |
| Caterer | Guest arrival, dinner service and cake times |
| DJ or band | Entrance, dances, speeches and song cues |
| Beauty team | Start time, order and number of people |
| Wedding party and parents | Where to be and when |

## Building in Buffers

Even well-planned days run late. Protect your schedule by:

- Adding 15 minutes to hair and makeup.
- Allowing extra travel time between locations.
- Planning a 10-minute break for yourselves after the ceremony.
- Deciding which moments can be shortened, such as portraits or cocktail hour, if needed.

## One Week Before

- Send the final timeline to all vendors and the wedding party.
- Confirm arrival times by phone or email.
- Print copies for the coordinator, venue and wedding party.
- Pack decor and supplies by area.
- Prepare tip envelopes and give them to a trusted person.

## Timeline Adjustments for Different Weddings

| Situation | How to adjust |
| --- | --- |
| No first look | Move couple portraits to cocktail hour and extend it to about 75 minutes |
| Morning ceremony | Start hair and makeup very early and plan a brunch or lunch reception |
| Two locations | Add travel time plus a 15-minute buffer for each move |
| Large wedding party | Add a second hair and makeup artist rather than starting earlier |
| Cultural ceremonies | Ask your officiant or family how long each ritual takes and add time between events |
| Winter wedding | Plan portraits before sunset, which can be early in the afternoon |

## Assign Day-Of Roles

Give trusted people clear jobs, written into the timeline:

- **Timekeeper:** keeps the day moving and cues key moments.
- **Family photo organiser:** gathers each family group for the photographer.
- **Vendor contact:** answers vendor questions so you are not interrupted.
- **Gift and card keeper:** collects cards and takes them somewhere safe.
- **Emergency kit holder:** stays close with the kit.
- **End-of-night lead:** packs decor, personal items and leftovers.

## Getting-Ready Space Checklist

- A room with good natural light for photos.
- Enough mirrors, chairs and power outlets for the beauty team.
- A clear rail or hook for the dress or outfit.
- Water, snacks and a simple lunch for everyone getting ready.
- A quiet corner for a few minutes alone before the ceremony.

## Common Timeline Mistakes

1. **No buffer time.** Small delays add up quickly.
2. **Too many photo groups.** Keep family photo lists focused.
3. **Forgetting travel time.** Traffic and parking take longer than expected.
4. **Not sharing the timeline.** Every vendor needs a copy.
5. **No one keeping time.** Assign a coordinator or a trusted friend.

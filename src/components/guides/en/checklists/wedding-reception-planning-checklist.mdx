## What a Great Reception Needs

A reception feels effortless when three things are in place: a clear order of events, smooth transitions between moments, and guests who are comfortable, fed and entertained throughout. This checklist covers the planning, the timeline and the details that make the evening run smoothly.

## Reception Planning Timeline

| When | Tasks |
| --- | --- |
| 10 to 12 months before | Book the venue, caterer, and DJ or band |
| 6 to 8 months before | Choose the reception style, menu direction and rentals |
| 3 to 4 months before | Menu tasting, cake order, decor and lighting plan |
| 6 to 8 weeks before | Choose music, speeches and special dances |
| 2 to 3 weeks before | Seating chart, final timeline, vendor confirmations |
| 1 week before | Final headcount, run-of-show to all vendors |

## Choose Your Reception Style

- **Plated dinner:** formal, guests stay seated, needs meal choices on RSVPs.
- **Buffet:** more choice and often lower staffing costs, but guests queue.
- **Family-style:** shared platters at each table, sociable and generous.
- **Food stations:** guests move around, good for mingling and mixed tastes.
- **Cocktail reception:** heavy canapés instead of a seated dinner, suits shorter or later events.

Choose the style that suits your venue, guest count and budget. Your caterer can advise on staffing and timing for each one.

## A Sample Reception Timeline

| Time | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 5:00 PM | Cocktail hour begins; couple's portraits |
| 6:00 PM | Guests seated; grand entrance |
| 6:10 PM | First dance |
| 6:15 PM | Welcome from a parent or the couple |
| 6:20 PM | Dinner service begins |
| 7:15 PM | Toasts and speeches |

| 7:50 PM | Cake cutting |
| 8:00 PM | Open dancing |

Adapt the times to your ceremony and venue rules. For the full day, combine this with the [wedding day-of timeline checklist](/checklists/wedding-day-of-timeline-checklist).

## Entrance, Dances and Special Moments

- Choose your entrance song and decide whether the wedding party is introduced.
- Pick songs for the first dance and any parent dances.
- Decide whether to hold the first dance straight after the entrance, which gets everyone's attention, or after dinner, which leads into open dancing.
- Plan cultural traditions, such as the hora, a money dance or a garter and bouquet toss, and tell the DJ or MC the order.
- Give the DJ a "do not play" list as well as a must-play list.

## Toasts and Speeches

- Choose who speaks: often a parent, the best man, maid of honour and the couple.
- Ask each speaker to keep to three to five minutes.
- Schedule speeches while guests are seated, between courses or after the main course.
- Make sure a microphone and speaker are ready and tested.
- Tell the photographer when speeches begin so they are in position.

## Food and Drink Details

- Hold a tasting and confirm the final menu.
- Include clear options for dietary needs and allergies.
- Plan the bar: full open bar, beer and wine, or a signature cocktail menu.
- Arrange water and soft drinks on every table.
- Plan a late-night snack for long receptions.
- Confirm meals for vendors who work through dinner.

## Decor, Lighting and Layout

- Request a floor plan from the venue showing tables, dance floor, bar, buffet and stage.
- Place the dance floor near the couple's table and away from the entrance.
- Choose centrepieces that do not block conversation across the table.
- Plan lighting: candles, string lights or uplighting change the mood as the evening goes on.
- Prepare signage: welcome sign, seating chart, bar menu and card box.

The [wedding decor and flowers checklist](/checklists/wedding-decor-flowers-checklist) lists every item for tables and rooms.

## Guest Comfort

- Seat older guests away from loud speakers.
- Provide a quiet space for guests who need a break.
- Make sure there are enough restrooms for your guest count.
- Plan for weather if any part of the reception is outdoors: heaters, fans, umbrellas or a tent.
- Arrange safe transport options at the end of the night.

## The Send-Off

- Decide how the evening ends: last dance, sparkler exit, petal toss or a private last dance.
- Check venue rules on sparklers, confetti and noise.
- Assign someone to collect gifts, cards and personal items.
- Confirm transport for the couple and any guest shuttles.

## Reception Day Checklist

- Confirm vendor arrival times and setup windows.
- Deliver decor, signage, favours and the guest book to the venue.
- Give the coordinator the seating chart and timeline.
- Brief the MC with names, pronunciations and running order.
- Test the microphone and music before guests arrive.
- Prepare tip envelopes and give them to a trusted person.

## Music Planning Checklist

- Choose songs for the entrance, first dance, parent dances, cake cutting and last dance.
- Give the DJ or band a must-play list of about 10 to 20 songs.
- Give a do-not-play list as well.
- Plan quieter background music for dinner, so guests can talk.
- Agree on when the dance floor opens and how to build energy.
- Share pronunciation of names for introductions.

## Reception Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| MC or DJ | Announcements, running order and keeping energy up |
| Coordinator | Timing, vendor questions and problem solving |
| Venue manager | Room, staff, power and safety |
| Trusted friend | Gifts, cards and personal items |
| Photographer | Being in place for every key moment |

## Reception Budget Checkpoints

Before you sign each reception contract, check these figures against your budget:

- Price per guest for food, including service charge and tax.
- Bar package price and how it is calculated (per guest, per hour or per drink).
- Rental costs for anything the venue does not supply.
- Overtime rates for the venue, DJ or band and photographer.
- Cake-cutting, corkage and vendor meal charges.

## Common Reception Mistakes

1. **Long gaps between moments.** Keep the timeline tight so energy does not drop.
2. **Too many or too long speeches.** Limit numbers and length.
3. **Dinner served too late.** Hungry guests leave early and dance less.
4. **No plan for vendor meals.** Many contracts require them.
5. **Forgetting the end of the night.** Someone needs to collect decor and gifts.

## Plan Your Decor Before You Buy Anything

Wedding decor works best when it follows a plan. Before booking a florist or buying a single candle, decide your colour palette, style and budget. A visual reference, such as a [wedding moodboard](/blog/moodboard-layout), helps every vendor understand your vision and stops you from buying items that do not match.

## Step 1: Set Your Style and Palette

- Choose two or three main colours and one accent colour.
- Describe your style in three words, such as "romantic, garden, soft" or "modern, minimal, bold".
- Consider the venue's existing colours, architecture and lighting.
- Think about the season and which flowers are naturally available.
- Collect 10 to 15 images that capture the look, and note what you like in each.

## Step 2: Know What the Venue Provides

Ask the venue for a written list of what is included:

- Tables, chairs and linens.
- Candles, lanterns or table numbers.
- Arches, backdrops or ceremony structures.
- Lighting and sound equipment.
- Rules on candles, confetti, petals, hanging decor and wall fixings.

Knowing the rules early saves money and avoids last-minute problems.

## Personal Flowers Checklist

- Bridal bouquet
- Toss bouquet, if you plan a bouquet toss
- Bridesmaid bouquets
- Boutonnieres for the partner, groomsmen, fathers and grandfathers
- Corsages or wrist flowers for mothers and grandmothers
- Flower crowns or hair flowers
- Flower girl basket and petals
- Flowers for the ring bearer or pet, if included
- Garlands or jaimala for cultural ceremonies

## Ceremony Decor Checklist

- Arch, chuppah, mandap or backdrop
- Aisle markers or aisle flowers
- Aisle runner, if allowed by the venue
- Chair decorations or pew ends
- Welcome sign and order of service sign
- Reserved seating signs for family
- Unity ceremony items, such as candles or sand
- Petal or confetti cones for the exit, if allowed
- Memorial table or display for loved ones

## Reception Decor Checklist

- Centrepieces for each guest table
- Head table or sweetheart table flowers
- Table numbers or names
- Place cards and escort cards or a seating chart display
- Menu cards
- Charger plates and napkins
- Candles, votives and lanterns
- Cake flowers or a cake topper
- Guest book table decor
- Bar and lounge area decor
- Restroom baskets with essentials

## Lighting Checklist

Lighting changes a room more than almost any other decor item:

- String lights or festoon lights for outdoor spaces
- Uplighting in your colour palette
- Candles for tables, with holders that meet venue rules
- Pin-spotting for centrepieces and the cake
- Lit signs or monograms

## Typical Decor Budget Split

| Area | Share of decor budget |
| --- | --- |
| Reception centrepieces and tables | 35 to 45 percent |
| Ceremony structure and aisle | 20 to 25 percent |
| Personal flowers | 15 to 20 percent |
| Lighting and candles | 10 to 15 percent |
| Signage and small items | 5 to 10 percent |

Adjust these shares to match your priorities. Track every quote in the [Wedding Budget Calculator](/tools/budget-calculator).

## Questions to Ask Your Florist

1. Which flowers in our palette will be in season on our date?
2. Can you suggest lower-cost substitutes that create the same look?
3. Can ceremony arrangements be moved to the reception, and who moves them?
4. What is included in delivery, setup and removal?
5. Do you rent vases, arches and candle holders, and what happens if they are damaged?
6. When must we confirm final quantities?
7. What happens if a flower is unavailable on the week of the wedding?

## Ways to Save on Decor

- **Repurpose:** move aisle arrangements to the head table or bar.
- **Go seasonal:** in-season flowers are cheaper and fresher.
- **Mix in greenery:** eucalyptus, olive and ferns fill space at lower cost.
- **Use candles:** a mix of candles and small bud vases looks generous for less.
- **Rent, do not buy:** rent arches, lanterns and vases instead of buying.
- **Focus on key areas:** spend most on the places that appear in photos, such as the ceremony backdrop and head table.

## DIY Decor Tips

- Choose projects that can be finished two or more weeks before the wedding.
- Make one sample first and get approval before making many.
- Assign a setup team and give them a photo of how each table should look.
- Pack decor in labelled boxes by area, such as "ceremony" or "table centrepieces".
- Plan who will collect and take home decor at the end of the night.

## Seasonal Flower Ideas

| Season | Flowers often in season |
| --- | --- |
| Spring | Tulips, peonies, ranunculus, sweet peas, lilac |
| Summer | Garden roses, dahlias, hydrangeas, sunflowers, lisianthus |
| Autumn | Dahlias, chrysanthemums, marigolds, amaranthus, berries |
| Winter | Amaryllis, anemones, hellebores, evergreens, white roses |

Seasons vary by region, so ask your florist what is local and fresh on your date.

## Decor Setup and Removal Plan

- Write a list of every decor item and where it goes.
- Take a photo of a sample table so the setup team can copy it.
- Assign one person to check the ceremony and reception rooms before guests arrive.
- Decide what happens to flowers after the event: give them to guests, family or a local care home.
- Pack labelled boxes for items that need to go home at the end of the night.

## Decor Delivery Day Checklist

- Confirm delivery and setup times with the venue and florist.
- Assign one person to receive deliveries and check them against the order.
- Keep flowers cool and out of direct sun until they are placed.
- Photograph each finished area for your records.
- Keep a small kit with scissors, tape, wire and spare candles.

## Common Decor Mistakes

1. **Centrepieces that block conversation.** Keep them low or very tall with a slim base.
2. **Forgetting venue rules.** Open flames and confetti are often restricted.
3. **Buying before the palette is set.** Mixed items rarely look cohesive.
4. **No plan for removal.** Someone must collect decor at the end of the night.
5. **Overlooking lighting.** Evening receptions need warm, layered light.

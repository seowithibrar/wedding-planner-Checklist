## Why a Master Guest List Matters

Your guest list touches almost every part of the wedding. It sets the size of the venue, the catering order, the number of invitations, the seating plan, the favours and even the number of chairs. A single, well-organised master list prevents the most common guest problems: invitations sent to the wrong address, forgotten plus-ones, missing meal choices and caterers working from the wrong numbers.

## What to Record for Every Guest

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Full name and household | Addresses invitations correctly and groups families together |
| Postal address | Needed for save-the-dates, invitations and thank-you notes |
| Email or phone | Fast follow-up for missing RSVPs |
| Tier (A or B list) | Controls who is invited in the first and second rounds |
| Plus-one status | Avoids confusion about who is invited |
| RSVP per event | Accurate numbers for each function |
| Meal choice and dietary needs | Required by the caterer |
| Table number | Used for escort cards and the seating plan |
| Gift received and thank-you sent | Keeps thank-you notes on track |

## Step 1: Agree on Numbers and Rules

- Set the maximum guest count based on venue capacity and budget per head.
- Agree how places are shared between the couple and families.
- Decide your plus-one policy and whether children are invited.
- Agree who has the final say when there is a disagreement.

Writing these rules down before anyone adds names keeps later conversations calm.

## Step 2: Build and Tier the List

- Collect names from both of you and from each family.
- Remove duplicates and combine households.
- Place each name in the A-list, B-list or C-list.
- Compare the A-list total against your maximum. If it is over, apply consistent cutting rules, such as "we have not spoken in two years" or "we have never met their partner".

Our article on [mastering your wedding guest list and seating chart](/blog/perfect-guest-list-guide) explains tiering and cutting rules in more detail.

## Step 3: Collect Addresses Early

- Send a short message or online form to collect addresses about eight to ten months before the wedding.
- Check spelling of names and titles.
- Note international guests, who need earlier mailings and travel information.
- Keep addresses in the same list as names so nothing is copied by hand.

## Step 4: Send Save-the-Dates and Invitations in Order

| Timing | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 6 to 8 months before | Save-the-dates to A-list guests |
| 8 to 12 months before | Save-the-dates for destination weddings |
| 8 to 10 weeks before | A-list invitations |
| As declines arrive | B-list invitations, until 6 to 8 weeks before |
| 3 to 4 weeks before | RSVP deadline |

## Step 5: Track RSVPs Carefully

- Number the back of each RSVP card discreetly to match guest rows, in case a card arrives without a name.
- Update the master list the day a reply arrives.
- Track replies per event for multi-day weddings.
- Record meal choices and dietary needs at the same time.
- Follow up personally with guests who have not replied two or three days after the deadline.

## Step 6: Track Dietary Needs and Accessibility

Caterers need accurate dietary information to plan safely. Use consistent labels: vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, dairy-free, halal, kosher and named allergies such as nuts or shellfish. Mark severe allergies clearly and give the caterer a list with names and table numbers.

Also note guests who need step-free access, seating near exits or restrooms, a quiet area, or a high chair for a young child. These details make a big difference to guests' comfort.

## Step 7: Build the Seating Chart

- Start when about 80 percent of RSVPs are in.
- Place the couple's table, parents and grandparents first.
- Group guests by family branch, friend circle and age group.
- Keep known conflicts at separate tables at similar distances from the couple.
- Seat guests with mobility needs near entrances and away from steps.
- Add meal choices to escort or place cards for plated dinners.

## Step 8: Send the Final Numbers

- Confirm the final headcount with the caterer and venue on the date in your contract.
- Send the seating plan to the venue or planner.
- Share the dietary list with table numbers.
- Give your stationer the final names for place cards and escort cards.
- Print a master copy for the coordinator on the day.

## Step 9: After the Wedding

- Record gifts as you open them.
- Send thank-you notes, ideally within two to three months.
- Keep the final list for future anniversaries and family events.

## How to Handle Last-Minute Changes

- **A guest cancels after the final headcount:** tell the caterer; you may still be charged, but they can adjust the seating.
- **A guest asks to bring someone at the last minute:** reply kindly and consistently with your plus-one rule.
- **A guest arrives who did not RSVP:** ask the venue to keep one or two spare place settings for this situation.
- **A table becomes too small:** keep the venue's maximum per table in mind and move a flexible guest rather than squeezing an extra chair.

## Common Guest List Mistakes

1. **Inviting more people than the venue can hold.** Always assume everyone could say yes.
2. **Inconsistent plus-one rules.** One exception quickly becomes many.
3. **Keeping several versions of the list.** Use one master list that everyone updates.
4. **Not recording meal choices with the RSVP.** Chasing them later wastes time.
5. **Leaving the seating chart to the final days.** It always takes longer than expected.

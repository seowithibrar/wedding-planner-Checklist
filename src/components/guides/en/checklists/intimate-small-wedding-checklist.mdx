## Why Choose an Intimate Wedding?

An intimate wedding swaps size for depth. With fewer guests, you can spend real time with every person, choose a venue that would be out of reach for a large wedding and invest in the details that matter most to you. Planning is often simpler, but the details are more visible, so each choice counts.

## Small Wedding Planning Timeline

| When | Tasks |
| --- | --- |
| 6 to 9 months before | Set budget, guest list, date and venue |
| 4 to 6 months before | Book photographer, caterer or restaurant, officiant and attire |
| 2 to 3 months before | Invitations, menu, flowers, personal touches |
| 1 month before | Licence, final details, seating, timeline |
| 1 week before | Confirm vendors, prepare letters and gifts, rest |

Small weddings can be planned faster than large ones. If you have only a few months, combine this list with the [3-month wedding planning checklist](/checklists/3-month-wedding-planning-checklist).

## Step 1: Define "Small" Together

- Agree on a firm guest number and stick to it.
- Decide the rule for who is invited, such as immediate family and closest friends only.
- Decide whether children and plus-ones are included.
- Discuss whether you will hold a larger, casual celebration later for everyone else.

## Step 2: Choose a Venue That Fits

Small groups open up more venue options:

- **Private dining rooms** at restaurants: food, service and setting in one booking.
- **Boutique hotels** and inns: ceremony, dinner and accommodation in one place.
- **Gardens and conservatories** for daytime ceremonies.
- **Historic houses, libraries or galleries** with character.
- **A rented home or villa** for a weekend celebration with close family.
- **Wineries and farms** for relaxed, scenic weddings.

Check minimum spends, which some venues apply even for small groups.

## Step 3: Make the Food the Centrepiece

With fewer guests, the meal often becomes the main event:

- Consider a chef's tasting menu or a family-style feast.
- Add wine pairings or a signature drink.
- Seat everyone at one long table to keep conversation flowing.
- Ask about personalised menus with each guest's name.
- Plan dessert as an experience, such as a dessert table or a small cake with a special flavour.

## Step 4: Personal Touches That Matter

- Write a short, personal note for each guest and place it at their seat.
- Choose place cards or menus with handwritten names.
- Invite guests to share a memory or blessing during dinner.
- Give a thoughtful favour that reflects your story or culture.
- Include meaningful family items, such as heirloom jewellery or photos of loved ones.

## Step 5: Book the Right Vendors

- Look for photographers who offer two to four-hour packages.
- Hire a florist for a few beautiful pieces rather than full room decor.
- Consider a solo musician or curated playlist instead of a full band.
- Choose stationery in small runs, or high-quality printed invitations since you need fewer.
- Confirm an officiant who is happy with a short, personal ceremony.

## Step 6: Plan a Relaxed Timeline

Intimate weddings have time for moments that are rushed at big weddings:

- Allow longer for portraits with each guest or family group.
- Leave time between courses for toasts and conversation.
- Consider a private first look or a walk together before dinner.
- Plan a gentle ending, such as dessert and drinks by a fire.

| Time | Event |
| --- | --- |

## Budget: Where to Spend and Save

| Spend more on | Save on |
| --- | --- |
| Food and drink quality | Large decor installations |
| Photography | Printed programs and extra signage |
| A special venue | Large dance floors and stages |
| Personal gifts and notes | Generic favours |

Use the [Wedding Budget Calculator](/tools/budget-calculator) to see how per-guest savings let you upgrade the things you care about.

## Handling Guests Who Expect an Invitation

- Announce early that you are having a very small wedding.
- Be consistent so nobody feels singled out.
- Share photos afterwards or host a video call during the ceremony for loved ones who cannot attend.
- Consider a casual reception, barbecue or dinner later for a wider group.

## Ideas for Meaningful Small Wedding Moments

- **A welcome circle:** start the day with every guest standing around you while the officiant shares a short welcome.
- **Shared vows:** invite parents or grandparents to say a few words during the ceremony.
- **A memory table:** display photos of family weddings across generations.
- **Story at dinner:** ask each guest to share how they met you, one at a time, during dessert.
- **A time capsule:** guests write letters to open on your first, fifth or tenth anniversary.
- **A private walk:** take ten minutes alone together after the ceremony before joining guests.

## Small Wedding Stationery Checklist

- Save-the-dates or a personal phone call
- Invitations with clear RSVP instructions
- A simple order of the day, printed or on a small card
- Personalised menus or place cards
- Handwritten notes for each guest
- Thank-you cards

With fewer guests, invest in high-quality paper or calligraphy for a few special pieces rather than printing many items.

## Small Wedding Guest Experience Checklist

- Greet every guest personally during the first hour.
- Plan seating so each guest sits near at least one person they know well.
- Offer a signature drink or a toast that includes everyone.
- Arrange transport or parking help if the venue is hard to reach.
- Prepare a short printed schedule so guests know what happens next.
- Thank each guest individually before they leave.

## Common Small Wedding Mistakes

1. **Letting the list grow slowly.** One "small" addition leads to many.
2. **Booking a room that is too large.** A half-empty room feels less intimate.
3. **Forgetting minimum spends.** Some venues require them regardless of guest count.
4. **Skipping a timeline.** Even small events need one for vendors.
5. **Not sharing the day.** Plan how to include people who were not invited.

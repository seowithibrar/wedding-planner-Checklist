## Is a Backyard Wedding Right for You?

A wedding at home or on a family property feels personal, relaxed and full of meaning. It also turns you into the venue manager. Everything a venue normally provides, from restrooms and power to parking, lighting and cleaning, becomes your responsibility.

Use this checklist to plan the infrastructure early, so the day feels effortless for your guests and your hosts.

## Start With a Site Assessment

Walk the property with your planner, caterer or rental company and answer these questions:

- **Space:** how many guests can be seated for dinner, with room for a dance floor, bar, buffet and catering area?
- **Ground:** is the ground level, firm and well-drained? Will grass need extra care or protection?
- **Access:** can delivery trucks reach the setup area? Are there gates, low branches or narrow paths?
- **Power:** where are outdoor outlets, and what is the electrical capacity?
- **Water:** is there access for catering and cleaning?
- **Neighbours:** how close are they, and how will noise, parking and lighting affect them?
- **Weather:** where will guests go if it rains, is very hot or gets cold after sunset?

## Infrastructure Checklist

| Item | What to plan |
| --- | --- |
| Tent | Size for seated dinner plus dance floor; sidewalls for rain; flooring if ground is uneven |
| Restrooms | Restroom trailer or portable units, placed near but not beside dining areas, with lighting |
| Power | Generator sized for catering, lighting, music and heating or cooling; cable covers |
| Lighting | String lights, path lights, restroom lighting and lighting for the catering area |
| Catering area | A flat, covered space with tables, power and water, out of guests' view |
| Water | Drinking water stations and handwashing |
| Waste | Trash, recycling and food waste bins, plus collection after the event |
| Parking | Parking area, attendants or a shuttle from a nearby car park |
| Climate | Heaters, fans, shade, umbrellas or blankets for the season |

## Permits, Insurance and Rules

- Contact your local council or city office about event permits, noise rules and amplified music limits.
- Check whether your tent size needs a permit or fire inspection.
- Ask about street parking rules and whether neighbours need notice.
- Check rules for open flames, sparklers and fire pits.
- Buy event liability insurance and ask whether your vendors need proof of it.
- If you rent a private estate, confirm what the owner provides and what you must arrange.

## Rentals Checklist

- Tables and chairs for ceremony and reception.
- Linens, napkins and table runners.
- Plates, cutlery, glassware and serving pieces, if the caterer does not supply them.
- Dance floor, stage or riser for musicians.
- Bar setup, ice and coolers.
- Lounge furniture for guests who are not dancing.
- Cake table, gift table and guest book table.

Ask the rental company for delivery, setup and collection times, and whether items must be cleaned before return.

## Vendor Coordination

- Choose a caterer experienced with off-site events who can bring field kitchen equipment.
- Ask every vendor for power, space and access needs.
- Share a site map showing setup areas, parking, restrooms and the catering zone.
- Confirm load-in and load-out times, especially if the property is someone's home.
- Hire a day-of coordinator. At home weddings, hosts often end up solving problems instead of celebrating.

## Guest Comfort

- Put clear signs for parking, restrooms and the ceremony area.
- Light paths and steps after dark.
- Offer water, shade and sunscreen for daytime weddings.
- Provide a sheltered, quiet area for older guests.
- Plan accessible routes for guests with limited mobility, avoiding soft grass and steps.
- Remind guests about footwear if the ceremony is on grass.

## Two-Week Countdown

- Confirm all rentals, tents and delivery times.
- Mow the lawn and trim plants a few days before, not the day before.
- Treat the area for insects if needed.
- Test the generator and lighting with your rental company.
- Prepare a rain decision time and tell vendors.
- Notify neighbours of the date and timing.
- Set up a lost and found and a first aid kit.

## The Day After

- Schedule collection of tents, rentals and restrooms.
- Arrange cleanup help or a cleaning crew.
- Return rental items according to the contract.
- Check the property for any damage covered by insurance.

## Sample Backyard Wedding Site Plan

Sketch your property and mark these zones before you talk to vendors:

| Zone | Placement tips |
| --- | --- |
| Ceremony | Level ground with an attractive backdrop, facing away from the afternoon sun |
| Cocktail area | Close to the ceremony, with shade and a drinks station |
| Dinner tent | Flat area with access for power and catering |
| Dance floor | Inside or beside the tent, away from neighbours' houses |
| Catering zone | Hidden from guests, close to the tent and water supply |
| Restrooms | Lit path, near but not next to dining, accessible for all guests |
| Parking | Separate from guest walkways; marked with signs |
| Vendor load-in | Clear access for trucks and vans, away from guest areas |

Share this plan with your rental company, caterer, photographer and coordinator so everyone knows where to go.

## Protecting the Property

- Lay ground protection under heavy equipment and tent legs where needed.
- Mark sprinkler heads, septic systems and underground cables before tent stakes go in.
- Move fragile garden items and furniture away from busy areas.
- Lock rooms in the house that guests should not enter.
- Agree in writing who pays for any damage caused by vendors.

## Backyard Wedding Budget Checklist

List these costs before deciding that home is cheaper than a venue: tent and flooring, restrooms, generator and fuel, lighting, tables and chairs, catering equipment, waste removal, parking help, insurance, permits and cleanup. Add a buffer of around 10 percent, because outdoor events often need extra items once vendors visit the site.

## Common Backyard Wedding Mistakes

1. **Underestimating costs.** Tents, restrooms and power can cost as much as a venue fee.
2. **Not enough power.** Tripped circuits during speeches are common without a generator.
3. **No rain plan.** A tent must be reserved, not decided on at the last minute.
4. **Poor lighting.** Guests need light to walk safely after sunset.
5. **No coordinator.** Hosts should be enjoying the day, not managing it.

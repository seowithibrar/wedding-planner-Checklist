A Christmas wedding gives you festive details and a beautiful backdrop, but your photos should still feel like the two of you. Use these poses as starting points, then adapt them to your outfits, venue, comfort, and schedule. The strongest gallery usually balances a few planned portraits with natural interactions and details from the day.


## By the tree and seasonal decor

### 1. The classic tree-side portrait

![The classic tree-side portrait at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-01-the-classic-tree-side-portrait.jpg)
Stand slightly angled toward each other beside the tree, with shoulders relaxed and hands joined. Look at your partner first, then turn toward the camera for one frame. This two-part setup gives your photographer both a tender interaction and a traditional portrait, while the decorated tree signals the season without dominating the picture.


### 2. Share an ornament

![Share an ornament at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-02-share-an-ornament.jpg)
Hold one meaningful ornament together and look at it rather than the camera. The small action gives your hands a natural place to rest and creates a detail connected to the day. Ask someone to bring the ornament from home, or choose one that matches the reception decor without making the pose feel like a product display.


### 3. Walk past the tree

![Walk past the tree at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-03-walk-past-the-tree.jpg)
Walk slowly past the tree while holding hands, keeping your attention on each other instead of the lens. The photographer can capture a wide view of the room and a closer frame as you pass. This works especially well when the reception is ready before guests arrive and the floor is clear.


### 4. Pause beneath a wreath

![Pause beneath a wreath at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-04-pause-beneath-a-wreath.jpg)
Stand together below a wreath or greenery arch and leave a little space around your heads so the decoration frames rather than crowds you. Turn toward each other for one image, then face forward. Check the placement before posing so no branch or ribbon appears to grow from either person’s head.


### 5. Look at the lights

![Look at the lights at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-05-look-at-the-lights.jpg)
Turn away from the camera and look at the lights together, standing close enough that the pose feels connected. Your photographer can make a wide image showing the room and a tighter portrait centered on your expressions. This is a comfortable choice for couples who prefer a quiet moment to a highly posed photograph.


### 6. Exchange a small gift

![Exchange a small gift at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-06-exchange-a-small-gift.jpg)
Give each other a small gift or handwritten note during a quiet pause before the reception. Keep the item personal and easy to hold, then focus on the exchange and reaction instead of looking directly at the camera. Tell your photographer beforehand so the moment can fit smoothly into the portrait schedule.


## Relaxed couple portraits

### 7. Forehead to forehead

![Forehead to forehead at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-07-forehead-to-forehead.jpg)
Stand face to face and gently touch foreheads, keeping your posture comfortable rather than leaning too far. You can close your eyes or smile softly at one another. Ask for a few frames before and after the pose, because the small laugh or breath afterward may feel more personal than a perfectly held expression.


### 8. A gentle cheek kiss

![A gentle cheek kiss at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-08-a-gentle-cheek-kiss.jpg)
Share a brief cheek kiss while your partner keeps their eyes open or rests their forehead near yours. The photographer can capture the moment from the side, then take a frame as you pull back and smile. Keep the pose affectionate in a way that feels natural to you; there is no need to exaggerate the gesture.


### 9. Walk toward the camera

![Walk toward the camera at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-09-walk-toward-the-camera.jpg)
Hold hands and walk toward the photographer at an easy pace, talking to each other as you move. Take several passes if needed, changing the direction of your gaze rather than stopping to pose. This creates varied expressions and shows movement in your clothes, while keeping the moment simple and comfortable.


### 10. Walk away together

![Walk away together at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-10-walk-away-together.jpg)
Walk away from the camera toward a doorway, garden path, or decorated reception room. This view can show the back of a dress, a veil, a coat, or the shape of both outfits. Choose a clear route and agree on where to stop, especially if the ground is wet or uneven.


### 11. Share a warm drink

![Share a warm drink at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-11-share-a-warm-drink.jpg)
Take a short break with cocoa, tea, or another drink that suits your celebration. Hold the cups naturally and talk to each other while the photographer captures a few frames. Confirm the cups are safe to handle with your outfits, and choose a quiet corner where guests and staff can still move freely.


### 12. Adjust a winter layer

![Adjust a winter layer at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-12-adjust-a-winter-layer.jpg)
Let one partner help the other settle a scarf, wrap, or coat before stepping outside. The gesture gives the photographer a natural interaction and documents a practical part of a winter wedding. Choose a layer that belongs with your outfit, and keep the moment brief so you stay warm and on schedule.


### 13. Show your joined hands

![Show your joined hands at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-13-show-your-joined-hands.jpg)
Bring your joined hands into the foreground and let your faces remain visible behind them. This can highlight rings without turning the whole portrait into a close-up of jewelry. Ask the photographer to capture a wider version too, so the image still shows your expressions and a little of the seasonal setting.


### 14. Sit together by a window

![Sit together by a window at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-14-sit-together-by-a-window.jpg)
Sit close on a stable bench or chair beside a window and turn slightly toward one another. The window can add soft daylight or show the winter scene outside, while your posture stays relaxed. Check that the seat is approved and comfortable, and avoid blocking a busy doorway or guest route.


## Celebration and movement

### 15. Take a slow first-dance turn

![Take a slow first-dance turn at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-15-take-a-slow-first-dance-turn.jpg)
Ask the DJ or band for a brief moment to take a slow turn together before the dance floor fills. Keep the movement small and comfortable, then let the photographer shoot through the turn instead of asking you to freeze. This gives you motion, connection, and reception light in a single series of images.


### 16. Raise your glasses for a toast

![Raise your glasses for a toast at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-16-raise-your-glasses-for-a-toast.jpg)
Stand close and raise your glasses toward each other for a quiet toast. Look at your partner first, then hold the glasses low enough that faces stay visible. Use drinks that are safe for everyone involved, and take the photograph before dinner service or speeches make the reception schedule tight.


### 17. Share a private laugh

![Share a private laugh at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-17-share-a-private-laugh.jpg)
Ask your photographer to prompt a favorite memory, a gentle joke, or a short conversation. Look at each other rather than trying to produce a big laugh on cue. The goal is not to perform for the camera; it is to settle into a genuine interaction that helps you feel at ease.


### 18. Step outside for a brief portrait

![Step outside for a brief portrait at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-18-step-outside-for-a-brief-portrait.jpg)
Choose a cleared, well-lit outdoor spot near the venue and step outside only if weather and footing are comfortable. Hold hands or stand close beneath an evergreen tree, then return indoors after a few frames. Keep coats available and skip the shot if the path is icy, dark, or crowded.


### 19. Pause at the doorway

![Pause at the doorway at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-19-pause-at-the-doorway.jpg)
Stand together at the venue entrance while warm light from inside frames you. One partner can look toward the guests while the other looks at them, creating two related images. Make sure the doorway remains clear for arrivals and accessible movement, and coordinate with staff before using the entrance. Keep the pose easy and comfortable to hold.


### 20. End with a quiet embrace

![End with a quiet embrace at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses/01-20-christmas-wedding-photo-poses-20-end-with-a-quiet-embrace.jpg)
Find a calm corner after the ceremony or near the end of the reception and share a comfortable embrace. Let your hands rest naturally and take a breath together while the photographer captures the moment from a little distance. This works as a closing image because it feels personal rather than carefully staged.


## Plan a photo list that fits your day

Choose a few favorite poses and share them with your photographer before the wedding. Add portrait time, local daylight, and any outdoor travel to your schedule, while keeping an indoor alternative ready.

A thoughtful family photo list helps you make time for important people without turning the portrait session into a long roll call. Adapt these combinations to your family, chosen family, traditions, and the people who are present. Share names and relationships with the photographer ahead of time, especially for blended families or sensitive groupings.


## Couple and close family

### 1. The couple with both sets of parents or guardians

![The couple with both sets of parents or guardians at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-01-the-couple-with-both-sets-of-parents-or-guard.jpg)
Gather the couple with the parents or guardians they want included, using names and relationships that feel right to everyone. Arrange people close enough for a warm group image while making each face visible. Ask about mobility needs and choose a level, comfortable location before calling the group together. Share names and preferred relationships before gathering people.


### 2. The couple with one partner’s parents or guardians

![The couple with one partner’s parents or guardians at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-02-the-couple-with-one-partner-s-parents-or-guar.jpg)
Take a separate portrait with one partner’s parents or guardians so the group can relax and connect naturally. Let the couple choose whether to stand between them or beside them. Share the names with the photographer in advance and avoid assuming a particular family structure or title. Let the family helper confirm everyone is present.


### 3. The couple with the other partner’s parents or guardians

![The couple with the other partner’s parents or guardians at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-03-the-couple-with-the-other-partner-s-parents-o.jpg)
Create a similar portrait with the other partner’s parents or guardians, keeping the pose and setting consistent if the couple wants a matched pair. Ask everyone how they prefer to be identified and make sure the photographer knows the names before the session begins. Use a clear, accessible spot for the group photo.


### 4. The couple with siblings from both families

![The couple with siblings from both families at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-04-the-couple-with-siblings-from-both-families.jpg)
Bring siblings from both sides together for a friendly portrait with the couple. Arrange the group in a comfortable line or shallow curve so everyone remains visible. If partners or children should be included, confirm the combination beforehand rather than making people decide while the photographer waits. Tell the photographer about any sensitive family dynamics beforehand.


### 5. The couple with one partner’s siblings

![The couple with one partner’s siblings at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-05-the-couple-with-one-partner-s-siblings.jpg)
Invite one partner’s siblings to stand or sit together with the couple. A natural conversation or shared glance can make the group feel less formal than a straight lineup. Ask about the order of the names and any preferred relationships so nobody is unintentionally left out. Arrange the group so everyone feels comfortable and included.


### 6. The couple with the other partner’s siblings

![The couple with the other partner’s siblings at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-06-the-couple-with-the-other-partner-s-siblings.jpg)
Take a matching portrait with the other partner’s siblings, using a similar background and light if possible. Let the family decide whether spouses or partners join. A clear list prevents confusion and helps the photographer move between combinations without repeating or missing the group. Confirm the requested groupings with a family helper.


### 7. The couple with grandparents who are attending

![The couple with grandparents who are attending at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-07-the-couple-with-grandparents-who-are-attendin.jpg)
Invite grandparents who are present to join the couple for a comfortable portrait. Choose a nearby chair or a location that does not require standing for long, and allow extra time to move. Keep the group small and warm, with the couple close enough to connect naturally. Share names and preferred relationships before gathering people.


### 8. The couple with children in the immediate family

![The couple with children in the immediate family at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-08-the-couple-with-children-in-the-immediate-fam.jpg)
Include children who are important to the couple in a short, relaxed portrait. Give them a simple role, such as standing beside the couple or holding a small approved prop. A parent or guardian should remain nearby, and the session should be brief enough to keep children comfortable. Let the family helper confirm everyone is present.


## Extended and chosen family

### 9. The couple with relatives from one side

![The couple with relatives from one side at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-09-the-couple-with-relatives-from-one-side.jpg)
Gather aunts, uncles, cousins, or other relatives from one side for a group portrait. Share the names and preferred grouping with a family helper before the wedding. Use a wide, level space so guests can arrange themselves without blocking access or standing too close to venue decor. Use a clear, accessible spot for the group photo.


### 10. The couple with relatives from the other side

![The couple with relatives from the other side at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-10-the-couple-with-relatives-from-the-other-side.jpg)
Take a corresponding photograph with relatives from the other side of the family. If the group is large, make one full image and then a smaller close-family version. Let the couple decide which relatives belong together rather than relying on assumptions about family roles. Tell the photographer about any sensitive family dynamics beforehand.


### 11. Both extended families together

![Both extended families together at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-11-both-extended-families-together.jpg)
Invite both extended families to join the couple in one large portrait if everyone is comfortable. Use a wide area and ask the photographer to check the composition before taking the image. A helper can make sure children and relatives who need a seat are placed comfortably. Arrange the group so everyone feels comfortable and included.


### 12. The couple with close friends who feel like family

![The couple with close friends who feel like family at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-12-the-couple-with-close-friends-who-feel-like-f.jpg)
Include close friends who have played an important family role in the couple’s life. Choose a small group and let everyone stand close or share a natural embrace if comfortable. Tell the photographer who should be included so the image reflects the relationships the couple values. Confirm the requested groupings with a family helper.


### 13. The couple with a mentor or family friend

![The couple with a mentor or family friend at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-13-the-couple-with-a-mentor-or-family-friend.jpg)
Invite a mentor, longtime family friend, or another important person for a dedicated portrait. This can be a meaningful way to recognize a relationship that might not fit a traditional family grouping. Ask the person beforehand and choose a quiet moment rather than pulling them away from a conversation unexpectedly.


### 14. The couple with relatives who traveled far

![The couple with relatives who traveled far at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-14-the-couple-with-relatives-who-traveled-far.jpg)
Gather relatives who traveled a long distance for a group image, if that feels meaningful to the couple. Let them know when and where photos will happen so they are not surprised. Keep the portrait short and comfortable, then let everyone return to the celebration. Let the family helper confirm everyone is present.


## Smaller groupings

### 15. Each partner with their own parents or guardians

![Each partner with their own parents or guardians at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-15-each-partner-with-their-own-parents-or-guardi.jpg)
Take an individual portrait of each partner with the parents or guardians they choose. This creates a quieter image and gives everyone a moment without the full group waiting. Use the preferred family terms and take both portraits in the same location if consistent lighting is important. Use a clear, accessible spot for the group photo.


### 16. Each partner with their own siblings

![Each partner with their own siblings at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-16-each-partner-with-their-own-siblings.jpg)
Photograph each partner with their own siblings, including partners or children if requested. A shared laugh or simple shoulder-to-shoulder pose can feel more personal than a formal lineup. Make sure the photographer has the correct names and order before the group gathers. Tell the photographer about any sensitive family dynamics beforehand.


### 17. Grandparents with their grandchildren

![Grandparents with their grandchildren at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-17-grandparents-with-their-grandchildren.jpg)
Create a portrait of grandparents with their grandchildren, with the couple nearby if desired. Use a stable chair and accessible location for anyone who needs one. Keep children close to a parent or guardian, and take the photograph early before young guests become tired. Arrange the group so everyone feels comfortable and included.


### 18. Siblings and their partners

![Siblings and their partners at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-18-siblings-and-their-partners.jpg)
Bring siblings and their partners together for a relaxed group image. The couple can join or step aside depending on which combination feels most meaningful. Arrange people so partners are included naturally and ask the family helper to confirm everyone is present before the photo begins. Confirm the requested groupings with a family helper.


### 19. The children in the family with the couple

![The children in the family with the couple at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-19-the-children-in-the-family-with-the-couple.jpg)
Gather children from the family for a short portrait with the couple. Keep the pose simple and allow them to stand, sit, or stay beside a familiar adult. Avoid asking children to hold still for several setups; one or two natural frames are often enough. Share names and preferred relationships before gathering people.


### 20. A final full-family portrait

![A final full-family portrait at a Christmas wedding](/images/blog/christmas-wedding-listicles/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations/10-20-christmas-wedding-family-photo-combinations-20-a-final-full-family-portrait.jpg)
Finish with a portrait of everyone the couple wants included, including chosen family and close friends. Ask the photographer to find a wide accessible spot and check that faces are visible before taking several frames. A family helper can call names and keep the gathering calm and quick. Let the family helper confirm everyone is present.


## Make the family-photo list easy to use

Order combinations to reduce movement and appoint one helper from each side who knows the names. Share pronunciation notes, mobility needs, and preferred groupings with your photographer. Keep the list near the [guest and seating plan](https://www.weddingplanningchecklists.org/blog/perfect-guest-list-guide) so important relationships are easy to track.

## How to Choose the Perfect Wedding Venue

Your venue sets the tone, date, and budget framework for your entire celebration.

<PlannerQuote author="David Sterling" role="Venue Procurement Specialist">
Never sign a venue contract until you've walked the physical space at the exact time of day your ceremony will take place to check sun angles and acoustics.
</PlannerQuote>

<CalloutBox type="warning" title="Hidden Fee Alert">
A base quote of $10,000 can easily reach $14,000 after 22% service fees, 8% state tax, and mandatory security guards are factored in.
</CalloutBox>

### Essential Site Tour Questions
1. Is our date available? What are peak vs off-peak rates?
2. What is the exact guest capacity for seated dinner vs cocktail hour?
3. What is the Plan B rain backup space for outdoor ceremonies?
4. Are there noise restrictions or early music cutoffs?
5. Where are the best natural lighting and portrait locations on site? (Take notes on scenic spots and sunset angles during your tour, then review our [wedding photography poses for beginners](/blog/wedding-photography-poses-for-beginners) to plan your couple and family photo sessions.)
6. Which vendors are required, preferred or not allowed? Can we bring our own caterer, bar service or decorator?
7. What time can vendors arrive to set up, and when must everything be cleared out?
8. What is included in the price: tables, chairs, linens, glassware, lighting, a sound system or staff?
9. What are the payment schedule, cancellation policy and rescheduling terms?
10. Is there parking, accessible entry and restrooms for guests with limited mobility?

## Step 1: Set Your Venue Budget Before You Search

The venue, together with catering, usually takes the largest share of a wedding budget, often around 40 to 50 percent combined. Decide on a maximum before you look, and include the extras that are easy to forget: service charges, taxes, security, cleaning, cake-cutting or corkage fees and any rentals the venue does not provide. Use the [Wedding Budget Calculator](/tools/budget-calculator) to see what your total leaves for the venue.

## Step 2: Know Your Guest Count First

Your guest count decides which venues are even possible. A venue that is too small feels cramped and may break fire safety limits, while one that is far too large feels empty. Build a draft list first, using our [guide to organising your guest list](/blog/perfect-guest-list-guide), then look for rooms that comfortably seat that number for dinner with space for a dance floor, a bar and a buffet if you need one.

## Step 3: Choose the Type of Venue

| Venue type | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hotel or banquet hall | All-in-one planning, large guest lists, guests who need rooms | Food and drink minimums, less flexibility on vendors |
| Restaurant | Smaller weddings, great food, simple planning | Limited space for dancing, fixed closing times |
| Barn or farm | Rustic style, outdoor ceremonies | Rentals, power, restrooms and weather back-up often extra |
| Garden or park | Natural backdrops and daytime ceremonies | Permits, weather, noise limits, limited shade |
| Historic house or museum | Distinctive photos and character | Strict rules on candles, decor and timings |
| Private home or backyard | Personal feel, flexible timing | Tenting, generators, restrooms, parking and insurance, covered in our [backyard wedding checklist](/checklists/backyard-wedding-checklist) |
| Destination resort | Weekend celebrations and travel | Guest travel costs and legal requirements, covered in our [destination wedding checklist](/checklists/destination-wedding-checklist) |

## Step 4: Shortlist Online, Then Tour in Person

Narrow your list to four to six venues using photos, reviews, capacity and published prices. Then visit in person. On each tour:

- Visit at the same time of day as your ceremony to see the real light.
- Walk the guest journey from parking to the ceremony, cocktail area, reception and restrooms.
- Ask to see the venue set up for another event, or ask for photos of a wedding with your guest count.
- Photograph every room and write notes immediately, because venues blur together after the third visit.
- Ask what other events might be happening at the same time on your date.

## Step 5: Compare Venues Like for Like

Quotes rarely use the same format. Build a simple comparison so you can see the true cost:

| Item | Venue A | Venue B | Venue C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base rental or minimum spend | | | |
| Catering per guest | | | |
| Service charge and tax | | | |
| Rentals not included | | | |
| Required extras (security, cleaning) | | | |
| Total estimated cost | | | |
| Capacity for seated dinner | | | |

Fill in the table during each tour. The venue with the lowest headline price is often not the cheapest once everything is included.

## Step 6: Read the Contract Carefully

Before you sign, check that the contract states clearly:

- Your exact date, start and end times, and the rooms or spaces included.
- The total price, everything included and every additional fee.
- The deposit amount, payment schedule and due dates.
- Cancellation and rescheduling terms, for both you and the venue.
- The rain plan and who decides when it is used.
- Rules on decor, candles, confetti, music volume and vendor access.
- Insurance requirements and any liability limits.

Ask for anything the coordinator promised verbally to be added in writing. Record each payment due date in the [Wedding Planning Calendar](/tools/wedding-planning-calendar).

## Red Flags to Watch For

- The venue will not give you a written, itemised quote.
- Reviews mention surprise charges, double bookings or rude staff on the day.
- The coordinator is vague about what happens if it rains.
- The contract allows the venue to change your room or date without your agreement.
- You are pressured to sign the same day with a "today only" price.

## When to Book Your Venue

Popular venues book 12 to 18 months ahead for Saturdays in peak season. If you have less time, look at Fridays, Sundays, weekdays or the off-season, and consider venue types that book up less quickly, such as restaurants or private spaces. Our [12-month wedding planning checklist](/checklists/12-month-wedding-planning-checklist) shows what to book straight after the venue, and the [3-month wedding planning checklist](/checklists/3-month-wedding-planning-checklist) explains how to find a venue on a short timeline.

## Questions to Ask About the Venue Team

The people who run the venue matter as much as the room itself:

- Will we have a dedicated coordinator, and will they be there on the day?
- How many weddings does the venue host each weekend?
- Who handles setup and cleanup, and what are the timings?
- Who do we call if something goes wrong on the day?
- Can we see the space during another event, or speak to a recent couple?

A responsive, organised venue team is a good sign of how your wedding day will be run. Slow replies and vague answers before you sign usually mean the same after you sign.

## Accessibility and Guest Comfort

Check that every guest can enjoy the day: step-free routes, accessible restrooms, seating for older guests during the ceremony, shade or heating, and enough parking or transport options. These details are easy to overlook on a tour and hard to fix later.

## After You Book the Venue

Once the contract is signed, share the venue details with your photographer, caterer and planner, ask for the floor plan, and note every deadline the venue gives you, such as the final headcount date and the last day to change the menu.

## Venue Selection Checklist

- Set a maximum venue budget, including fees and taxes.
- Agree on a draft guest count.
- Decide on your preferred season, day of the week and two or three backup dates.
- Choose two or three venue types to consider.
- Shortlist four to six venues online.
- Tour each venue at your ceremony time.
- Ask every site tour question and record the answers.
- Compare full costs in one table.
- Read the contract, request written changes and confirm insurance requirements.
- Pay the deposit and add every payment date to your calendar.
